Healthier habits

Small changes can make social media more helpful and less overwhelming. The goal is not to avoid it completely, but to use it with control and clear boundaries.

Manage notifications

Turn off alerts that are not important so the phone does not interrupt your attention all day. Fewer notifications can make it easier to focus and reduce the urge to check apps constantly.

Follow better accounts

Choose creators and pages that teach, inspire, or support your goals instead of filling your feed with noise. A more intentional feed can make your time online feel more useful and less stressful.

Set time limits

Use screen-time limits or a schedule so app use stays under control and does not take over the day. Research shows many teens already feel they spend too much time on social media, and some are actively trying to cut back.

Daily routines

Healthy habits work best when they are part of a routine. A few simple rules can make social media easier to manage without making it feel restrictive.

No-phone moments

Keep phones away during meals, homework, family time, and the first few minutes after waking up. These breaks create space for better focus and real-life conversation.

Bedtime boundaries

Avoid scrolling right before bed because it can make it harder to relax and fall asleep. Studies connect heavy social media use with poorer sleep quality and later bedtimes.

Scheduled check-ins

Instead of checking apps all day, set a few times to look at messages and updates. This helps social media fit into your day instead of interrupting it.

Better screen balance

Balance matters because social media is only one part of life. Time offline can improve focus, sleep, and overall well-being when it replaces endless scrolling.

Replace scrolling

When you catch yourself opening an app out of habit, try reading, walking, listening to music, or talking to someone instead. Replacing the habit is often more effective than trying to simply stop it.

Track your use

Watching your daily app time can help you notice patterns and spot the moments when you use social media most. Tracking makes it easier to set realistic goals.

Take regular breaks

Short breaks from social media can reduce overload and help you reset your attention. Even small pauses can make a difference when they happen consistently.

Simple rules to try

Start with one or two changes instead of changing everything at once. A realistic habit is more likely to last than a strict plan you cannot maintain.

Rule 1: Turn off nonessential notifications.
Rule 2: Avoid social media 30 minutes before bed.
Rule 3: Check apps only at set times during the day.
Rule 4: Unfollow accounts that make you feel stressed or distracted.
Rule 5: Replace one scrolling session with an offline activity.
